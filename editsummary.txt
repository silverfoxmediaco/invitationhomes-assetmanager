[2026-09-21] Project created
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
~/Documents/InvitationHomes/invitationhomes-foundry, with CLAUDE.md and this
file as the first two.

WHAT THIS IS
  A management and C-suite operations platform for a single-family rental
  portfolio, on Palantir Foundry. Ontology first, React front end later,
  developed locally in VS Code against the OSDK.

  Five domains: inventory management; rental payment tracking; maintenance;
  property tax tracking; and expenses, rental history and market rate comps
  with trending rates.

THE COMPANY IS REAL, THE DATA IS NOT
  Invitation Homes (NYSE: INVH) is a public single-family rental REIT. Every
  figure in this project is generated. The framing rule carried over from
  Target Air: present it as "here is how I would model your problem", never as
  their actual operations and never as SFR experience James does not have.
  Synthetic data gets labelled as synthetic everywhere a reader will see it.

THE THESIS — THE JOIN
  Property -> Lease -> RentPayment -> Expense / MaintenanceWorkOrder / TaxBill,
  with MarketRateComp hanging off the property's submarket.

  It answers: which homes are underperforming their market rate, what is that
  costing, and is it a pricing problem or a cost problem. A property management
  system knows the lease says $2,150. A market feed knows comparable homes now
  go for $2,480. The ledger knows this home absorbed $6,400 of maintenance and
  a 12% reassessment. None of them alone distinguishes "raise rent at renewal"
  from "this asset is a problem". Everything else is supporting cast, and
  ambiguous modelling decisions get resolved in favour of keeping that chain
  traversable.

GROUNDING PULLED FROM invitationhomes.com (2026-09-21)
  19 markets, named in CLAUDE.md. Leases 12-24 months. ProCare maintenance with
  24/7 emergency service. Lease Easy bundle (smart-home tech, air filter
  delivery, utility management). Up to 3 pets. Online applications and payments.

  CORRECTION WORTH KEEPING: the per-market counts on their site (Atlanta 810,
  Tampa 598, Phoenix 508 and so on) are homes CURRENTLY AVAILABLE TO LEASE, not
  homes owned. The real portfolio is on the order of 80,000. Sizing the
  synthetic set off the availability numbers would model a company roughly two
  orders of magnitude too small.

CARRIED FORWARD FROM TARGET AIR
  The Foundry gotchas are in CLAUDE.md rather than being rediscovered: action
  types absent from the SDK until registered and regenerated; Vite's dependency
  pre-bundle cache; LocalDate vs instants on action parameters; "Allow edits"
  before any write action; bulk ontology deletes being a trap; v1 vs v2 CSV
  upload and the schema-less raw upload serving stale data.

  Also the data-generation discipline: deterministic seed, shape the data so
  the interesting case is genuinely there, reconcile derived values after
  generation rather than during, and re-check the data once pages render it.
  Both Target Air data bugs were invisible in the CSVs and obvious on screen,
  because nothing errors on data that is valid and wrong.

OPEN
  - Portfolio scale for the synthetic set: how many properties, across how many
    of the 19 markets.
  - Whether this has a real audience (pitch, interview, client) or is a
    portfolio piece. Decides how much front-end polish is warranted.
  - Whether market comps need a plausible external source modelled, or a
    generated submarket rate curve is enough.

NEXT
  Settle scale and the object list, then write the generator.

THREE THINGS THE DATA REVEALED — all of them affect the model

1. "MARKET" IS AN OPERATING REGION, NOT A METRO. The tampa market contains Fort
   Myers, Cape Coral, Port Charlotte, Venice, Lake Alfred and Haines City —
   Southwest and Central Florida, some of it 100+ miles from Tampa. So market is
   too coarse a key for rental comps: a Fort Myers comp is not a Tampa comp.
   Comps must key on city or ZIP/submarket, NOT on market, or the comp engine
   will quietly compare homes in different housing economies.

2. CITY NAMES COLLIDE ACROSS STATES. "The Reserve" sits in Dallas, GEORGIA
   (atlanta market) while a separate dallas market exists in Texas. Any join
   keyed on city name alone silently merges a Georgia community into the Texas
   market. City must always be qualified by state. This is the same shape as the
   Target Air BOM bug: valid data, wrong data, nothing errors.

3. THEIR SOURCE DATA HAS DEFECTS, which is worth knowing when the synthetic set
   claims to mirror it. "Clarksvile" is misspelled on their page (normalized to
   Clarksville here). Vineland Reserve is written "Osteen, Florida" where every
   other row uses a two-letter code, which is why it was the one row that failed
   state extraction (normalized to FL).

EXTRACTION NOTE: innerText returns '' for elements that are not rendered, so an
innerText-based scrape found all 76 links but no city/state for 68 of them.
textContent works on hidden nodes but concatenates without separators, running
name and city together. Fix was to take the SHORTEST descendant whose text ends
in ", XX" as the location and subtract it from the anchor's full text.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISIONS TAKEN: communities portfolio only, 3 years of history, ~3,000

and behind-on-rent are visible.

ANSWERING "how many properties do we have data on": ZERO. We have 76
COMMUNITIES. The pages publish no home counts either (checked). Everything below
the Community line is generated, and that line stays visible in the build.

TWELVE OBJECTS  Community (76, real) and County; Property (~3,000); Resident
(~4,000), Lease (~4,500), RentPayment (~108,000); MaintenanceWorkOrder
(~27,000), Vendor, Expense (~30,000); TaxAssessment and TaxBill (~9,000 each);
MarketRateComp (~13,000).

THREE MODELLING DECISIONS THAT CARRY WEIGHT
  Residents persist ACROSS leases. A renewal is a second lease for the same
  resident. A resident whose history lives inside one lease cannot be "at risk"
  in any useful sense, and a renewal must not reset a record to clean.
  Comps key on CITY, not market — tampa spans $1,759-$2,919 because it covers
  Fort Myers and Port Charlotte. Market-level comps would report under-market
  gaps that are only geography.
  Delinquency buckets computed at READ time from daysLate, so thresholds can
  change without regenerating 108,000 rows.
  Accepted denormalisation: Property carries city/state/countyId although all
  three are reachable via Community, because the comp join and tax rollup run
  per property on every render. Cost: those fields must stay consistent with the
  community. Raise it unprompted rather than waiting to be asked.

AT-RISK COHORT SHAPED, NOT SPRINKLED  ~8% of residents DEGRADE (on-time -> late
-> partial -> missed) rather than being uniformly bad; a flat random sprinkle
makes everyone look alike and leaves nothing to find. ~1.5% progress to
terminated-early or evicted so the chain from behaviour to lease outcome to turn
cost is traceable. Some at-risk residents sit in under-market homes and some do
not — if the two problems always coincided the dashboard could not show they are
different questions, which is the thesis.

AIP SECTION ADDED after James asked whether AIP needs all this data. Two things
travel under that name with opposite requirements:
  AIP Logic / agents / NL questions reason at query time, do NOT learn from the
  data and do NOT need volume. They need an ontology that DESCRIBES ITSELF.
  Build rule recorded: every object type, property and link gets a written
  description AS IT IS CREATED, saying what a field means and its units rather
  than restating its name. Retrofitting descriptions across 12 object types is
  the kind of job that never gets done.
  Trained models need volume, history and labels — which the spec supports — but
  A MODEL TRAINED ON SYNTHETIC DATA CANNOT BE VALIDATED BY IT. The generator
  creates the at-risk cohort with an explicit rule; any model will recover that
  rule and score beautifully. The accuracy figure measures how well it
  reverse-engineered our own generator. NEVER present a model accuracy number
  from this data — anyone with ML experience sees the circularity at once and it
  would discredit the parts of the prototype that are real. What IS honest to
  demonstrate is the PIPELINE: ontology supports feature engineering, model
  trains, predictions write back to objects, UI surfaces them, user acts.
  AT-RISK SCORE SHIPS RULES-BASED, not ML: arrears bucket 40, recent trend 25,
  direction 15, payment ratio 10, lease stage 10; bands healthy / watch / at
  risk / critical. Transparent, works day one, explainable in a sentence, and
  what property managers actually use. Direction is its own signal because a
  resident who always pays on day 3 is not the same risk as one who paid on time
  for a year and has now missed twice.
  AIP Logic then sits ON TOP for narrative — summarise why a resident is flagged
  and what the exposure is — reading the score rather than computing it, which
  keeps the number auditable and the explanation fluent.

first dashboard; county assignment needs a real ZIP -> county geocode because the
page coordinates are a default map centre and unusable.

THREE BUGS FOUND BY LOOKING AT THE OUTPUT, NOT BY READING THE CODE
1. 17 of 2,997 properties priced BELOW their community's "starting at" floor.
   The 0.99 tail of the rent jitter pushed smallest-bed/smallest-sqft homes
   under the anchor, where the size factor was exactly 1.0. Caught by
   validate.py. Fixed with an explicit max() against the anchor — "starting at"
   is a floor and nothing may sit beneath it.
2. LEASE SEASONALITY WAS INVERTED. October held 30.8% of all lease starts and
   May-Aug only 15.7%, the exact opposite of the spec. Cause: every property
   began its lease history at START (2023-10-01) and the nudge loop meant to
   scatter them could only step 10-40 days at a time, so it never escaped the
   pile-up. Fixed by choosing each property's FIRST lease start from a
   season-weighted window reaching up to two years BEFORE our reporting window —
   a real portfolio is already mid-tenancy when reporting opens. Payments are
   still only emitted from START. October fell to 11.1%, May-Aug rose to 36.6%.
3. That fix introduced a NEW failure: leases starting before property
   acquisition, because month_iter yields month STARTS which can precede the
   window's own lower bound. Caught by validate.py on the very next run. Fixed
   by clamping the cursor to `earliest`.

Then May-Aug still sat at 37% against a 45% target. Two further changes:
  - turn gap now samples 4 realistic vacancy lengths (5-75 days) and prefers the
    one landing in a better leasing month. Snapping straight to the next peak
    month would have invented months-long vacancies instead.
  - 18-month terms made rare rather than 1-in-6. 12 and 24 preserve the start
    month; 18 shifts it by six, walking a summer lease into winter on every
    renewal. Their published range is "12 to 24 months" and whole-year terms
    dominate in practice.
  Result: May-Aug 41.0%. The residual gap is genuine drift from turnover, which
  is realistic — forcing it to exactly 45% would mean faking the turn cycle.

SEASONALITY VERIFIED IN THE OUTPUT, not assumed
  on-time rate   Jan 83.7% (lowest), Dec 86.7%, Sep 86.8%, Apr peak 91.9%

912 rows, $1.78M. Expense gains `scope` (property|community) and `communitySlug`;

THE VALIDATOR EARNED ITS KEEP AGAIN. My first version of the utilities check was
a lazy ratio threshold with a dead loop in it — it passed. Rewritten to actually
assert what it claimed (every utilities row must fall outside every lease term
for its own property), it immediately failed 5,052 rows. Cause: month_iter
yields month STARTS, and the first one falls on or before the vacancy's own
start, i.e. inside the previous lease. Fixed by requiring the month's 1st to
land strictly inside the gap. A weak check is worse than no check, because it
buys false confidence.

[2026-09-21] Link types: a name collision, and a Foundry gotcha worth keeping
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Building Communities -> Counties, the link API name `county` came back INVALID.
Cause: Communities already has a PROPERTY whose API name is `county` (the county
name string). A link type's API name shares a namespace with the object type's
property API names, so they collide.

ATTEMPTED FIX THAT DID NOT WORK, and the lesson in it:
  Renamed the property's DISPLAY name from "County" to "County Name" — which is
  more accurate anyway, since the field holds a name. Foundry then warned:
  "This edit leaves the display name no longer matching the API name. The API
  name was not updated automatically, because renaming one is a breaking change
  for existing consumers."
  So DISPLAY NAME AND API NAME ARE INDEPENDENT. Renaming the display name does
  NOT free up the API name, and I told James it would. It did not.
  The icon beside that warning is "ask AIP Assist", not a quick-fix.
  Could not find anywhere in the property editor to edit the API name directly:
  General tab has name/description/base type/value type/status; the "..." menu
  offers only Details and Advanced, and Advanced is schema migrations. It may
  not be editable in the UI at all, consistent with Foundry treating it as a
  breaking change.

RESOLUTION: keep the display-name fix (it was worth making on its own terms) and
name the LINK `countyRecord` instead. Reads fine as community.countyRecord.get().
The only link of the fifteen with a clash — Properties has only `countyId`, no
`county` name column, so Properties -> Counties takes plain `county`.

FOR NEXT TIME: check for property/link API name collisions BEFORE creating object
types, not while building links. Any object type carrying both a `<thing>` name
string and a link to `<Thing>` will hit this.

[2026-09-21] The dashboard rendered and immediately exposed a generator bug
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
First run of the overview against live Foundry data: auth, CORS and the join all
worked, and the screen said 2,512 of 2,761 occupied homes were under market. 91%.
That is not a finding, it is a flat line, and a CFO would spot it in seconds.

DIAGNOSIS — gap to market by lease start year:
  2024  +8.8%   2025  +8.0%   2026  +7.8%
Every cohort under by the same amount, INCLUDING leases signed this year. A
lease signed last month is by definition at market, so a constant offset meant
the gap was an artifact rather than a fact about the portfolio.

CAUSE. Leases were priced off a single fixed property.marketRent while comps
were generated separately AT THE END with a 6-13% upward drift. The gap was
therefore the drift itself, applied uniformly, and had nothing to do with when a
lease was signed.

FIX. One market curve per (city, state, beds), monthly across the window, built
BEFORE properties. Everything now prices off it:
  - a home's asking rent = curve at AS_OF x a per-home size premium
  - a lease's rent      = curve at ITS OWN START MONTH x that same size factor
  - the published comps = the curve itself
So a home's rent and its benchmark cannot drift apart by construction.
Renewals rise 1-5% and may close part of the gap, capped so they cannot leap to
market or exceed +12% year on year — which is what leaves long-tenured residents
genuinely under-rented, the population the dashboard exists to surface.

RESULT  46.3% of active leases under market, and the gap now RISES with age:
  2024 median +5.3% (90% under)  2025 +1.6% (63%)  2026 -2.4% (28%)
That is a cohort effect a CFO can act on, not a constant.

SECOND BUG, CAUGHT BY validate.py NOT BY EYE. The curve covered 2-7 bedrooms;
nine homes in Crandall TX have ONE bedroom, from a community whose scraped range
starts at 1. They ended up with no benchmark at all. The check "every property
has a comp for its city+beds" failed and named them. Widened to 1-7.

This is the third time the same discipline has paid: build the thing that
renders the data, then look at it. Nothing errored in either case.
